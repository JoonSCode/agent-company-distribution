# Marketing Method Lenses

Use this reference to choose a lens, resolve a conflict, or support a strategic
rationale. It is a decision map, not a checklist or ranking of celebrities.

## Contents

1. Source and selection rules
2. Strategy and demand
3. Brand, creative, and persuasion
4. Growth and distribution
5. Measurement
6. Tension-resolution rules

## Source And Selection Rules

- Prefer observed customer behavior, repository evidence, analytics,
  experiments, and author- or institute-owned sources.
- Treat practitioner frameworks as lenses with limits, not universal laws.
- Treat vendor guidance as useful but interested evidence.
- Use the smallest lens set that changes the decision.
- The targeted additions below adapt research checked on 2026-10-26; they are
  not validated prompts, guaranteed outcomes, or execution authority. Reuse
  existing decision fields rather than adding framework artifacts.
- Keep source-derived claims in `known`, synthesis in `inferred`, and unresolved
  applicability in `unknown`.

## Strategy And Demand

### Mark Ritson: Diagnosis Before Strategy Before Tactics

Use for market orientation, segmentation, target choice, positioning,
objectives, and preventing channel-first planning.

- Operational rule: map the market before choosing where and how to win.
- Limit: heavyweight segmentation is disproportionate when a small app lacks
  data; use the lightest diagnosis that changes a choice.
- Sources: [Tactics without strategy](https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-beware-the-tactification-of-marketing/),
  [Brand diagnosis](https://minimba.com/articles/the-missing-step-in-marketing-plans-brand-diagnosis).

### April Dunford: Positioning As Context

Use when customers cannot quickly understand why to choose the product; skip
repositioning when the live task is settled copy craft.

- Inspect competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, customer
  value, best-fit segments, and market category.
- Build messaging and storytelling only after positioning.
- Limit: do not invent a new category by default; test and refresh positioning
  as markets and alternatives change. Its B2B origins do not settle consumer
  habit or emotional context. Observed confusion, a changed real alternative,
  or weaker downstream user quality can reverse the selected context.
- Sources: [Introduction to positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/an-introduction-to-positioning),
  [Quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning).

### Clayton Christensen, Bob Moesta, Chris Spiek: Jobs To Be Done

Use to understand why people switch, stall, or continue non-consumption; skip
another interview framework when relevant behavior already explains the choice.

- Inspect the struggling moment, purchase timeline, current workaround, real
  alternatives, desired progress, pull, push, anxiety, and habit.
- Prefer recent switch interviews and observed behavior over stated feature
  preferences.
- Limit: JTBD does not replace targeting, commercial validation, or market
  sizing. Retrospective accounts are recall-biased; observed use that conflicts
  with the inferred job reopens the hypothesis rather than redefining the user.
- Sources: [Christensen on JTBD](https://hbr.org/podcast/2020/01/revisiting-jobs-to-be-done-with-clayton-christensen),
  [Re-Wired guide](https://therewiredgroup.com/learn/complete-guide-jobs-to-be-done/),
  [JTBD interview method](https://jobstobedone.org/),
  [Christensen Institute](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).

### The Mom Test: Past Behavior, Not Compliments

Use when demand evidence consists of praise or future purchase promises; skip
new interviews when observed switching, purchase or retained use already answers
the decision. Recent concrete behavior and workarounds can reduce leading-question
bias, but recall and sample selection still limit inference. Contradictory usage
or credible commitment evidence changes the demand judgment; an interview alone
does not establish market prevalence. Existing outreach and privacy authority
still applies. Source: [author's introduction](https://www.momtestbook.com/)
(not a claim to have read or validated the full book).

### Seth Godin: Smallest Viable Audience And Permission

Use for the first learning audience, trust, community spread, and owned
communication.

- Choose who the work is for and create enough value that they return or share.
- Treat permission as an earned promise, not a legalistic email entitlement.
- Limit: a narrow audience is an early focus mechanism, not a permanent excuse
  to avoid broader category growth.
- Sources: [Smallest viable audience](https://seths.blog/2022/05/the-smallest-viable-audience/),
  [Permission marketing](https://seths.blog/2008/01/permission-mark/).

## Brand, Creative, And Persuasion

### Byron Sharp And Jenni Romaniuk: Availability And Distinctive Assets

Use for category buying situations, recognition, reach, and ease of purchase.

- Build mental availability around category entry points.
- Build physical availability through presence, prominence, and a buyable
  portfolio.
- Preserve brand elements that are both recognizable and uniquely linked.
- Limit: broad-reach evidence from established categories does not remove an
  early app's need for a narrow learning wedge and budget realism.
- Sources: [Category entry points](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world),
  [Distinctive assets](https://marketingscience.info/learn-with-us/commercial-research/distinctive-asset).

### Les Binet And Peter Field: Time Horizons

Use when short-term activation threatens to crowd out future demand.

- Separate immediate response from longer-term business and brand effects.
- Select allocation based on stage, category, margin, cash, buying cycle, and
  measurement power.
- Limit: never hard-code the famous 60:40 heuristic for a personal app.
- Sources: [Effectiveness in context](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/effectiveness-in-context-reviewed-by-michael-lee),
  [Channel mix](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/how-channel-mix-impacts-marketing-effectiveness).

### Claude Hopkins: Traceable Direct Response

Use for offers, response paths, specific claims, and disciplined comparison.

- Make the offer and expected action concrete.
- Trace cost and response, and compare tests before scaling.
- Limit: immediate response misses some future demand and historical examples
  require modern ethical and statistical review.
- Sources: [Scientific Advertising](https://www.scientificadvertising.com/original/),
  [book contents](https://books.google.com/books?id=aUeCmpbkfO0C).

### David Ogilvy And Modern Ogilvy: Central Idea And Brand Cues

Use for creative briefs and multichannel coherence.

- Ground creative in research and a strong central idea.
- Preserve recognizable cues while customizing execution to each channel.
- Give each channel a clear role.
- Limit: famous quotations are heuristics, not campaign evidence.
- Sources: [Ogilvy principles archive](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/ogilvy-75-quotations-david-ogilvy),
  [Multichannel principles](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/five-principles-optimize-multichannel-campaigns).

### Joanna Wiebe: Conversion Copy From Customer Evidence

Use when writing landing pages, App Store assets, emails, ads, or offers.

- Research and discover, write and edit, then validate and experiment.
- Mine interviews, reviews, support, competitor alternatives, product data,
  and analytics for message hierarchy and customer language.
- Limit: page conversion can rise while lead or user quality, retention, trust,
  or brand falls.
- Sources: [Conversion copy process](https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/what-is-conversion-copywriting/),
  [Research techniques](https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/copywriting-research/).

### Robert Cialdini And Rory Sutherland: Behavioral Influence

Use for ethical persuasion, framing, and small behavioral interventions.

- Cialdini lenses: reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking,
  social proof, and unity.
- Sutherland lens: perceived value and reframing can change behavior without a
  large functional change.
- Limit: require truth, user benefit, reversibility, and a falsifiable outcome.
  Never fabricate urgency, proof, authority, or consent.
- Sources: [Cialdini principles](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/),
  [Sutherland TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/rory_sutherland_life_lessons_from_an_ad_man).

## Growth And Distribution

### Brian Balfour, Casey Winters, Andrew Chen, Elena Verna: Growth Systems

Use for product-channel fit, retention, compounding mechanisms, and growth
model diagnosis.

- Connect product, channel, market, and monetization instead of planning them
  in silos.
- Define the core value action and its natural cadence.
- Treat retention and engagement as growth foundations.
- Model a loop only when one cohort or action measurably creates the next.
- Use a funnel to diagnose loss within a journey, not to claim a compounding
  company growth system.
- Limit: not every app has virality or another strong loop.
- Sources: [Growth loops](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops),
  [Retention](https://www.reforge.com/blog/retention-engagement-growth-silent-killer),
  [Four Fits](https://www.reforge.com/blog/four-fits-growth-framework).

### Gary Vaynerchuk: Channel-Native Social Execution

Use only after audience, message, and channel role are clear.

- Adapt the central idea to the native grammar of each platform.
- Provide repeated value before asking for action.
- Limit: content volume and platform attention are tactics, not strategy or
  proof of business value.
- Source: [Gary Vaynerchuk books](https://garyvaynerchuk.com/books/).

### Apple: App Store Decision Surface

Use for iOS product pages, audience-message matching, and store experiments.

- Test icons, screenshots, and previews through Product Page Optimization.
- Use Custom Product Pages for audience- or feature-specific continuity.
- Compare downloads with activation, retention, proceeds, and paying-user
  quality where available.
- Limit: store conversion is not incrementality; low traffic can make results
  inconclusive.
- Sources: [Custom Product Pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/),
  [Product Page Optimization](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/).

## Measurement

### Causal Lift, Marketing Mix Modeling, And Attribution

Choose the method from the decision:

- randomized or controlled lift: estimate what marketing caused;
- A/B surface test: compare a specific intervention when assignment is valid;
- cohort and retention analysis: inspect downstream user quality;
- marketing mix modeling: estimate portfolio-level contribution over time;
- attribution: describe credited touchpoints, not causal truth.

Record power, confidence, comparison validity, conversion lag, and opportunity
cost. Label underpowered or observational results as directional.

Use a controlled incrementality test when a causal allocation choice needs it
and a valid, authorized comparison is feasible; skip causal estimation for a
settled copy edit or a small explicitly directional learning test. The benefit
is separating credited demand from additional demand, not guaranteeing lift.
The [eBay field experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20171) illustrates
selection bias; its result is not an app forecast. Valid assignment/exposure
evidence, a changed baseline or downstream-quality harm can reverse the
allocation judgment. An underpowered result remains inconclusive.

Sources: [Google Conversion Lift](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12005564?hl=en),
[Google measurement stack](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16719772?hl=en),
[Meridian MMM](https://developers.google.com/meridian).

## Tension-Resolution Rules

| Tension | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Narrow audience vs broad reach | Start narrow for learning and first access; broaden around real category buying situations after retention and message evidence improve. |
| Differentiation vs distinctiveness | Require both a credible reason to choose now and a consistent way to recognize the brand. |
| Brand building vs direct response | Label each activity's time horizon; measure response without treating last-click results as total value. |
| Funnels vs loops | Use funnels for local journey loss and loops only for measured replenishment. |
| Persuasion vs trust | Truth, permission, user benefit, reversibility, and downstream quality are hard constraints. |
| Vendor metrics vs causal proof | Use platform data for operations; use valid experiments or calibrated models for causal allocation claims. |
| Framework completeness vs decision speed | Select the minimum lens set that can reverse or improve the live decision, then stop. |

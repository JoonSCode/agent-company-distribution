# Brand Voice And Localization

Use this reference to define a voice system, adapt tone to context, or make copy
natural across locales.

## Voice And Tone

`voice` is the relatively stable way a product communicates. `tone` changes
with the user's context, emotional state, stakes, and channel.

Define voice as behavioral ranges, not adjectives alone:

| Dimension | Preferred behavior | Avoid | High-stakes adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity | familiar, concrete language | jargon and abstraction | become more explicit |
| Warmth | respectful and human | forced intimacy | reduce playfulness |
| Confidence | grounded in evidence | hype and certainty inflation | name unknowns |
| Energy | purposeful rhythm | noise and exclamation | become calm |
| Expertise | explain the useful model | patronizing detail | prioritize action and safety |

For each voice attribute, include:

- what it means in behavior;
- a `do` example;
- a `do not` example;
- where the rule bends;
- prohibited claims or tones.

## Tone Matrix

Adjust tone by moment:

- success: concise acknowledgment; delight only when proportional;
- first use: welcoming and orienting, not overly familiar;
- confusion: patient and specific;
- error: calm, non-blaming, recovery-focused;
- destructive or financial decision: direct and explicit;
- privacy or permission: transparent and agency-preserving;
- incident: factual, accountable, and update-oriented;
- promotion: energetic only within evidence and brand bounds.

Never change terminology, product truth, or consequence to make tone friendlier.

## Terminology

Maintain:

- preferred product and feature names;
- one term per concept and action;
- reserved system or platform terms;
- prohibited, deprecated, ambiguous, and legal-review terms;
- capitalization, numerals, dates, units, and punctuation conventions;
- glossary notes for translators.

Do not create a synonym merely to avoid repetition in interface copy. Repetition
can improve recognition when it preserves the same concept.

## Localization Principles

- Translate meaning, user intent, and consequence rather than English syntax.
- Use natural target-language information order.
- Preserve product terminology only when it is intentional and recognizable.
- Avoid idioms, cultural references, humor, slang, and wordplay unless the
  locale can recreate the same job safely.
- Plan for text expansion and contraction.
- Keep placeholders and variables semantically clear with example values.
- Do not concatenate sentence fragments or assume English word order.
- Localize dates, time, currency, decimals, plural rules, honorifics, names,
  addresses, and units.
- Test truncation, wrapping, control growth, bidirectional layout, VoiceOver or
  screen-reader pronunciation, and large text.

## Korean Copy

For Korean product copy:

- write natural Korean rather than preserving English noun stacks;
- omit subjects and pronouns when Korean naturally does so and meaning remains
  clear;
- choose one speech level and keep it consistent within the product;
- prefer concrete verbs over excessive nominalization such as `진행`, `수행`,
  or `처리`;
- avoid literal translations of `Get started`, `Unlock`, `Experience`, and
  `Learn more`; write the actual action or value;
- keep particles outside variables only when every substituted value makes the
  grammar valid; otherwise rewrite the sentence;
- test numerals, counters, spacing, line breaks, and mixed Korean-English
  product terms on the real surface;
- preserve calm, explicit language in permission, payment, deletion, privacy,
  and error moments.

Natural localization may be longer or shorter than the source. Brevity does not
justify translated-sounding Korean.

## Inclusive Language

- Name people by the identity relevant to the task, not a stereotype.
- Avoid assuming gender, marriage, family structure, ability, nationality,
  literacy, device access, or financial status.
- Use disability language appropriate to the community and context.
- Avoid metaphors that stigmatize disability, illness, poverty, or mental
  health.
- Review examples, names, imagery references, and cultural events across
  locales.

## Voice Evaluation

Review:

- recognizability without a logo;
- clarity when the playful wording is removed;
- consistency of terms and action labels;
- fit with the user's emotional state;
- naturalness in the target locale;
- product truth and evidence;
- accessibility and pronunciation;
- resilience under long content and variable substitution.

Do not approve a voice from adjective lists alone. Use frozen interface,
support, conversion, error, and incident cases.

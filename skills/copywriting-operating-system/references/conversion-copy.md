# Conversion Copy

Use this reference after Marketing has settled target choice, positioning,
objective, non-goals, channel job, allowed claims, and evidence.

## Required Inputs

- reachable target or learning audience;
- buying or switching situation;
- real alternatives, including doing nothing or a workaround;
- differentiated capability and supported value;
- product and offer truth;
- objections and evidence;
- primary objective and action;
- channel role and surface constraints;
- downstream-quality and trust guardrails.

If an input could reverse the message, return to Marketing.

## Research Before Drafting

Prefer:

- recent interviews and switching stories;
- reviews, support, search, and community language;
- observed behavior and funnel evidence;
- reasons for purchase, refusal, cancellation, refund, and churn;
- competitor and workaround language;
- verified product data and demonstrations.

Separate customer language from the writer's interpretation. A frequent phrase
is not automatically a promise the product can support.

## Message Architecture

Build in this order:

1. context: the situation and alternative the reader recognizes;
2. problem or desired progress;
3. value: why this product is relevant now;
4. mechanism: how the product creates that value;
5. proof: evidence appropriate to the claim;
6. objection: the highest-friction uncertainty;
7. offer or terms;
8. action and expectation after the action.

Not every surface needs every layer. Preserve the hierarchy even when the
artifact is short.

## Headlines And Leads

- Make the central idea understandable without the body.
- Lead with customer value or a meaningful product truth, not internal
  taxonomy.
- Prefer specificity over superlatives.
- Avoid vague transformations such as `revolutionize`, `unlock your potential`,
  or `the ultimate` unless the surrounding evidence makes a precise meaning
  clear.
- Do not write a headline that outruns screenshots, product behavior, terms, or
  downstream experience.

## Claims And Proof

For each material claim, record:

| Claim | Claim type | Evidence | Certainty | Surface limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is being promised | capability, outcome, comparison, privacy, social proof, price | product, research, analytics, policy, external source | observed, supported, directional, hypothesis | where and how it may be used |

Rules:

- Capability proof does not automatically prove a user outcome.
- Observational conversion does not prove incrementality.
- A synthetic test does not prove market effectiveness.
- One testimonial does not establish a general result.
- A percentage, ranking, comparison, guarantee, or privacy statement requires
  the exact source, population, window, and exclusions needed to interpret it.

## Calls To Action

- Name the action and expected next state.
- Match commitment level: `See how it works`, `Start trial`, `Buy`, and
  `Subscribe` are different promises.
- Put price, renewal, trial, cancellation, and eligibility terms where the
  decision requires them.
- Do not use urgency unless it is real, material, and time-bounded.
- Do not make the safer or declining option intentionally obscure.

## Persuasion

Use relevance, specificity, demonstration, credible proof, risk reduction, and
clear terms before behavioral influence techniques.

Scarcity, authority, social proof, reciprocity, consistency, liking, and unity
are ethical lenses only when:

- the underlying fact is real;
- it helps the reader judge fit;
- it does not exploit vulnerability or obscure a choice;
- the intervention can be falsified and monitored for harm.

## Variants And Experiments

Create variants only when each represents a distinct hypothesis, for example:

- problem-led versus desired-progress-led framing;
- mechanism-led versus proof-led support;
- concrete utility versus emotional identity;
- low-commitment exploration versus direct purchase.

Keep the audience, offer, product truth, layout, and exposure mechanism fixed
unless the experiment is explicitly designed to test them. Define success,
failure, inconclusive handling, downstream quality, and trust guardrails before
launch.

Clicks, views, conversion rate, and attribution are not enough when activation,
retention, refund, complaint, unsubscribe, or paid-user quality can reverse the
decision.

## Practitioner Lenses

Use each lens for its bounded job:

- April Dunford: establish positioning context before messaging.
- Jobs to Be Done: inspect switching situations and desired progress.
- Joanna Wiebe: research customer language, draft, edit, and validate conversion
  copy.
- Claude Hopkins: make offers and response paths specific and traceable.
- David Ogilvy: preserve one central idea and recognizable brand cues.
- Cialdini: inspect ethical influence mechanisms without fabricating evidence.
- Binet and Field: separate immediate activation from longer-horizon demand;
  never hard-code a universal allocation.

These lenses guide synthesis. They do not prove audience, claims, causality, or
commercial fit.

## Conversion Review

Reject the copy when:

- the reader cannot identify the product, value, or next action;
- the promise is broader than the proof;
- important terms appear after commitment;
- urgency, scarcity, proof, or authority is fabricated;
- the copy optimizes response at the expense of user quality or trust;
- the landing, store, paywall, and product experience make materially different
  promises;
- the variant cannot teach a decision because too many variables changed.

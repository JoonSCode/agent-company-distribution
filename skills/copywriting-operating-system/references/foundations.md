# Copy Foundations

These are stable decision principles. They outrank stylistic trends.

## Purpose

Copy should help a person understand the situation, decide with appropriate
information, and complete or recover from a task. It is part of product and
service design, not a final layer applied after the system is built.

## Principles

### Start From The User Need

- State the task, question, risk, or desired progress the person has now.
- Include what the person needs to act; remove what only the organization wants
  to say.
- If the content cannot be justified by a user or business decision, omit it.

### Prefer Clarity Over Cleverness

- Lead with the essential meaning.
- Use familiar, specific words and concrete verbs.
- Write for scanning before close reading.
- Use humor, metaphor, novelty, and wordplay only after meaning is unmistakable
  and stakes are low.

### Make Every Action Honest

- Label a control by its actual result.
- State material consequences before commitment.
- Distinguish continue, save, submit, buy, delete, cancel, and close.
- Never use a vague positive label to conceal a financial, privacy, destructive,
  or irreversible action.

### Preserve Agency

- Give meaningful choices where the product supports them.
- Make decline, defer, cancel, undo, and recovery understandable.
- Do not shame, pressure, obstruct, or create false urgency.
- Explain why information or permission is needed at the point of decision.

### Speak From Product Truth

- Support every capability, outcome, comparison, privacy, and proof claim.
- Match certainty to evidence: `does`, `can`, `may`, and `designed to` are not
  interchangeable.
- Separate a product mechanism from a hoped-for user outcome.
- Do not promote a prototype, roadmap, or synthetic test as shipped behavior or
  observed effectiveness.

### Respect The Reader's State

- Adapt tone to emotion and stakes.
- Avoid celebration during failure, humor during distress, and promotional
  language during support or recovery.
- Do not blame the user. State what happened, what remains safe, and what can be
  done next.

### Design A Hierarchy

- Put the decision or action before supporting detail.
- Give one component one primary message.
- Use headings, labels, helper text, and links for distinct jobs.
- Move optional detail to progressive disclosure when the surface supports it.

### Be Consistent, Not Robotic

- Use one term for one product concept and one action.
- Preserve brand voice while changing tone for context.
- Keep repeated controls and states identifiable across the product.
- Break a house rule when clarity, safety, accessibility, or local language
  requires it; record the reason.

### Include Everyone

- Use person-first, respectful, non-stereotyping language.
- Avoid unnecessary gender, age, ability, culture, and family assumptions.
- Do not encode meaning only through visual location, color, imagery, or idiom.
- Treat readable labels, instructions, error identification, error suggestion,
  alternative text, and assistive-technology names as content responsibilities.

### Write For Translation And Growth

- Avoid idioms, puns, slang, noun stacks, ambiguous pronouns, and unexplained
  abbreviations when they are not essential.
- Keep strings semantically complete; do not build sentences from fragments
  that translators cannot reorder safely.
- Plan for expansion, pluralization, grammatical gender, dates, numbers,
  currencies, and bidirectional text.
- Prefer natural localization over word-for-word preservation of the source.

## Ethical Persuasion

Persuasion is acceptable when:

- the claim is true and proportionate;
- the choice benefits or at least does not exploit the user;
- material terms are visible;
- the action is reversible when feasible;
- the outcome can be evaluated;
- downstream quality and trust are measured with response.

Reject fabricated scarcity, social proof, authority, testimonials, comparison,
countdowns, guarantees, or personalized claims. Reject confirmshaming,
preselected consent, hidden costs, disguised ads, obstruction, nagging, and
cancellation friction.

## Conflict Resolution

When principles conflict, use this order:

1. safety, truth, legal and platform compliance;
2. user understanding and agency;
3. accessibility and localization;
4. task completion and recovery;
5. brand consistency;
6. brevity, persuasion, novelty, and trend fit.

Concise copy that hides necessary meaning is not clear copy.
